## 1 — Wheel slip was invisible (detector built and checked against two bags)

**The issue.** 2026-11-17: wheels spun for ~16 s at the runner's edge, odometry counted
distance that did not happen, and nothing in the stack could tell.

**The idea.** The walls do not lie. If two scans half a second apart line up with no
shift, the robot did not move, whatever the encoders say.

**The method** (`scan_motion.py`):

1. `scan_to_points` (`:68`) — ranges to xy points, undoing the smear from turning while
   the lidar sweeps (0.1 s per turn; at 1 rad/s that is 6°, or 30 cm at 3 m).
2. `estimate_translation` (`:112`) — the rotation between the scans is *taken* from the
   gyro; only the shift is solved, by sliding the new points onto the old scan's wall
   lines. The start guess is "no movement" on purpose: seeding with wheel odometry
   would bias the answer toward the thing being checked.
3. `forward_distance` (`:168`) — lidar shift to `base_link` forward travel (the lidar
   sits 5 cm ahead, so turning swings it sideways).

**The trap, and how it is closed.** In a plain corridor nothing constrains motion along
it; a naive matcher reports "0 cm" — the slip signature. `forward_support` counts the
points that really pin down forward motion, and the rule refuses to judge below 20:

```python
# scan_motion.py:185-193
SLIP_MIN_WHEEL_SPEED_MPS = 0.08     # below this there is nothing to check
SLIP_MAX_SPEED_RATIO = 0.5          # walls saw < half of what the wheels claim
SLIP_MIN_FORWARD_SUPPORT = 20.0     # else the verdict is "unknown", never "slip"
SLIP_CONFIRM_WINDOWS = 2            # one low window (pulling away) is not slip
```

`tests/test_scan_motion.py::test_plain_corridor_reports_no_forward_support` builds that
corridor and checks it.

**Verified.**

- 2026-11-17 bag (the slip): `S` from 18.1 s, about 1 s after the wheels started
  spinning; the real driving at 34-41 s all `ok`. Wheels 3.30 m, walls 1.67 m.
- 2026-11-18 nav run (the control): trip 1 out and back all `ok` (three single
  `suspect` windows, never confirmed). Trip 2: slip confirmed at 68.7 s — within about
  a second of the dresser contact in the phone video.
- 2026-11-18 mapping drive: 4 minutes, zero slip windows.
- Standing still the lidar speed reads 0 ± 0.01 m/s; one comparison costs ~7 ms on the
  workstation (not timed on the Jetson).

**Live.** The node ran on the robot for the two later missions (section 7, 8): 145 `ok`,
4 `suspect`, 0 `slip` on the clean run.

**Not done.** It only publishes and logs (`/robot_001/slip/status`, `lidar_speed`,
`phantom_m`). Nothing acts on a slip verdict; whether to stop, re-localize, or feed a lidar speed into the EKF is undecided.

**Lesson.** "Wheels turning, walls not moving" is also what being stuck against
something looks like. The same signal is a cheap bump detector.

## 2 — The home error (measured, explained)

**The issue.** After trip 1 the robot was "close but not on" its start spot; trip 2 then
hit the dresser beside the bedroom door. Same family as the 0.55 m on 2026-11-16.

**How it was found.** Three independent measurements, none using the robot's own
position estimate:

1. *Scan before trip 1 vs scan after it, matched directly:* 0.55 m from the start spot
   (0.29 m ahead, 0.47 m right), heading 34° clockwise. Already 0.48 m off before the
   final spin; the spin itself moved the robot 6 cm.
2. *Scans drawn on the borrowed map:* at the marker 73-87% of points land on a mapped
   wall; at home 18%, and no nearby pose does better than 34%
   (`reports/robot_boot_20261118_navonly/overlay_start_marker.png`).
3. *Scans located in the new map (section 3):* the hand-placed start is the same spot on
   both of tonight's placements, within 3 cm and 2°. Anchored on the bedroom walls, that
   spot is ~0.6 m from the configured home pose — which, in the real house, lies on a
   hallway wall.

**The chain.** AMCL starts 0.6 m wrong. In the bedroom the map is right, so AMCL
corrects itself (`map→odom` moved 0.5 m in x during trip 1). "Return home" then goes to
the configured home *relative to the bedroom*, a different physical place from the
start (the sideways 0.47 m). Back in the hallway the wrong walls pull the estimate
around (the remaining ~0.5 m along the hall, and the heading). Trip 2 starts from a
pose the robot believes is right, and the same planned path is shifted into the
dresser, whose corner is 0.15 m from where the robot stopped.

**Not the cause tonight:** wheel odometry (wheels and walls agreed on trip 1), the final
spin (6 cm).

**A mistake of mine, caught before it reached a conclusion.** My first analysis script
applied the 5 cm lidar offset with the wrong sign, which made the final spin look like
a 25 cm shift and briefly suggested the lidar was mounted in the wrong place. The
tested module (`forward_distance`) had it right and the scratch script disagreed with
it; fixing the script made the two spins read 6-7 cm.

**Lesson.** When localization is good in one room and bad in another, draw a scan on the
map in each before suspecting sensors. One picture settled it.

## 3 — The robot's own map (A3: built, installed, proven in section 7)

**The drive.** `scripts/robot_map_drive.sh` on the Jetson: bag recorder, driver layer
(no camera), then `key_teleop.py` in the foreground. Mike drove 4 min 11 s, 14.6 m,
ending 0.16 m and 11° from the start.

**Hold-to-move, and why.** A command lives 0.6 s after the last key byte
(`key_teleop.py:40`), long enough to bridge a terminal's pause before key repeat, short
enough that a frozen ssh session or dropped WiFi ends in "stop". There is no
keep-driving mode: this robot has no collision avoidance.

**The build, offline.** `scripts/build_map_from_bag.sh <bag>` runs slam_toolbox in its
own DDS domain and feeds it from the bag with `tools/map_from_bag.py`, which does three
things `ros2 bag play` cannot:

```python
# tools/map_from_bag.py
resample_scan(...)        # :59  LD19 scans have 500-505 readings; Karto needs a fixed count
DeadReckoner(...)         # :84  no EKF on a mapping drive: rebuild odom from wheel speed + gyro
resting_gyro_offset(...)  # :109 measure the gyro offset from the bag's stand-still stretches
```

The dead reckoning *starts at the configured home pose*, so the map comes out in the
mission's frame: home in the map is `nav2_params.yaml`'s AMCL initial pose and
`semantic_map`'s `home_base`. No waypoint and no sim file changes.

**Verified (offline).**

- A map built from wheel speed + gyro alone matches one built from the EKF's own TF:
  98% of wall cells within one 5 cm cell.
- Scans located in the final map: mapping-drive start at (-1.27, 1.21, 90°) with 99%
  of points on walls; the nav run's start at (-1.25, 1.23, 88°); its marker stop at
  (1.15, 3.35), 99%.
- `home_base` has 0.43 m to the nearest wall, the marker goal 0.70 m; a 0.24 m-radius
  robot can get from one to the other, with the door as the pinch (about 8 cm spare
  each side).

**Not committed, on purpose.** The map is a floor plan of a real home and the repo is
public. It is in `~/fleet-ci-data/maps/` on the Jetson; `robot_boot.sh:257-267` mounts
it read-only into the container, or falls back to `living_room.yaml` with a loud
warning. Half a map (one of the two files) is never mounted.

**A mistake of mine.** The first `robot_map_drive.sh` passed `camera_launch_file:=''`,
which `ros2 launch` rejects as malformed; the driver layer died at once and the script
sat waiting while Mike's keys were echoed by the terminal. I had tested that the script
refuses to run without a terminal, not the launch line itself. Fixed by not passing the
argument (empty is the default) and by failing at once if the drivers exit during
start-up (`robot_map_drive.sh:106-`); then proved on the Jetson with a 14 s no-motion
launch before handing it back.

**Lesson.** Run the one line that touches hardware before asking someone to stand next
to the robot.

## 4 — Gyro offset was never learned during teleop (fixed)

**The issue.** In the mapping-drive bag the gyro read +0.27°/s standing still; in the
nav run, 0.00. Over four minutes that is ~65° of false heading in the raw odometry.

**Cause.** `esp32_driver` learned the offset only when the watchdog had tripped (nobody
publishing). The teleop publishes zero-speed commands at 20 Hz while idle, so the
driver never considered the robot stopped.

**The fix.**

```python
# esp32_driver.py:141 (in _cmd_vel_cb)
self._cmd_is_zero = (msg.linear.x == 0.0 and msg.angular.z == 0.0)
# esp32_driver.py:302-303 (in _publish_imu)
commanded_still = self._stopped or self._cmd_is_zero
stationary = (info.speed_l == 0 and info.speed_r == 0 and commanded_still)
```

**Verified.** Two new tests in `tests/test_esp32_driver.py` (zeros arriving → learned;
a real move commanded with wheels momentarily at zero → not learned). Not separately
checked on the robot. For tonight's map the offset was measured from the bag (+0.263°/s) and
subtracted offline.

## 5 — A new node would have broken the driver layer on the Jetson (prevented)

**The issue, found before the push.** The Jetson's bare-metal install is a
`--symlink-install` build from 2026-08-11 and `hil_stage.sh sync` no longer builds.
Edited Python files reach it through symlinks, but a *new* console script
(`slip_monitor`) has no launcher until a rebuild, and `drivers_only_launch.py` would
fail on it.

**The fix.** `scripts/ensure_nav_fleet_built.sh` compares `setup.py`'s entry points
with `install/nav_fleet/lib/nav_fleet/` and runs
`colcon build --symlink-install --packages-select nav_fleet` if one is missing; no
launcher after the build means no mission. `robot_boot.sh:62` and `robot_map_drive.sh`
call it before sourcing the install.

**Verified.** Three tests against stand-in `colcon` (no rebuild when current; rebuild
happens before the drivers; a failed or useless rebuild stops the run). On the Jetson:
it rebuilt in 9.7 s, produced `slip_monitor` and `key_teleop`, a second call was silent
and the checkout stayed clean.

## 8 — Carpet run: two clean trips, then the robot could not be stopped

**What happened.** Runner back down, same map. Trips 1 and 2: home error 0.07 and
0.04 m, zero slip — the carpet was not a problem. Trip 3 reached the marker; as the
return leg began (a spin on the spot, on the bedroom carpet) the robot "started doing
circles and could not recover". Mike's kill command did not work; both of my ssh
sessions timed out; he switched the robot off at its power switch. Tyres were found
partly off their rims.

**How the cause was found.**

1. Every file on the Jetson (bag, driver log, mission log) ends at 19:20:36-37, the
   second the return leg started, and its content stops about 15 s earlier (unflushed
   writes). A Jetson that had merely lost WiFi would have kept writing for minutes. The
   Jetson itself stopped.
2. Read straight from the motor board afterwards, no motion commanded: `"v": 917`. The
   firmware source confirms the unit (`(int)(loadVoltage_V * 100)`): **9.17 V**, about
   3.06 V per cell. Flat.
3. So, as a reading of the evidence rather than a measurement: the spin pulled the pack
   low enough to switch the Jetson off; the motor board, which tolerates less, stayed up.
4. Why the board kept going — this part is in the firmware source, not an inference:

```c
// waveshareteam/ugv_base_ros, ROS_Driver/uart_ctrl.h
case CMD_SPEED_CTRL:  ... heartbeatStopFlag = false;      // T:1  re-arms the timer
                          lastCmdRecvTime = millis();
                          setGoalSpeed(L, R);
case CMD_ROS_CTRL:    rosCtrl(X, Z); break;               // T:13 does neither

// ROS_Driver/movtion_module.h
void heartBeatCtrl() {
  if (currentTimeMillis - lastCmdRecvTime > HEART_BEAT_DELAY) {   // 3000 ms default
    if (!heartbeatStopFlag) { heartbeatStopFlag = true; setGoalSpeed(0, 0); }
  }
}
```

The driver sent T:13. With T:13 the timer fires once, 3 s after power-on, sets its flag,
and is never re-armed. From then on the board holds its last command for as long as it
has power. That has been true on every run since August; this was the first time the
Jetson died with a motion command active.

**What I had wrong.** The project's notes (and I, to Mike, tonight) said the board stops
by itself within 3 s of silence and that losing the ssh session stops the robot. The
first came from reading the source of a *different* firmware variant in August and was
never tested on the robot. The second is true only while the Jetson is alive and the
connection closes cleanly: a dropped link leaves the session open on the Jetson, so no
SIGHUP arrives.

**Lesson.** A safety stop that has never been seen to work is an assumption. Test the
stop that covers "the computer is gone" by actually taking the computer away, on a
stand, before the robot is ever on the floor.

## 9 — The fix for section 8 (tested; bench proof still owed)

**Wheel speeds instead of a twist.** Same arithmetic the firmware applies to T:13, done
in the driver, sent as T:1 so every command re-arms the board's timer:

```python
# esp32_protocol.py
def twist_to_wheel_speeds(linear_x, angular_z, track_width):
    half = angular_z * track_width / 2.0
    return linear_x - half, linear_x + half          # firmware: A = X - Z*W/2, B = X + Z*W/2

def encode_wheel_speed_cmd(left_mps, right_mps):
    return {"T": 1, "L": float(left_mps), "R": float(right_mps)}   # floats: is<float>() check
```

`esp32_driver._send_motion` is now the only place a motion command is built; moves,
stops and the watchdog's stop all go through it.

**The board's timeout is set at every start-up** (`firmware_stop_ms`, default 500;
`{"T":136,"cmd":500}`). It is not stored in flash. 500 ms is ten command periods at
20 Hz and 15 cm of travel at 0.3 m/s.

**Verified.** `tests/test_esp32_driver.py::test_motion_is_never_sent_as_t13...`,
`::test_startup_sets_the_boards_own_stop_on_silence_timeout`, and the protocol tests.
**Not verified on the robot.** The proof is the bench test, wheels off the ground:

```bash
# on the Jetson, esp32_driver NOT running, robot on a stand
python3 tools/bench_stop_on_silence.py                # must print PASS
python3 tools/bench_stop_on_silence.py --legacy-t13   # the old command: expect FAIL
```

It drives both sides slowly for 2 s, then sends nothing — no stop — and watches the
wheel speeds the board reports. PASS = they reach zero by themselves within the timeout
plus 0.7 s.

**Still not covered:** a Jetson that is alive but has lost WiFi. The mission continues
(that is correct for an unattended robot) and an attended operator has no remote stop.

## 10 — Battery: measured all along, never looked at (fixed)

**The issue.** Two sessions in a row ended on a flat pack. Both looked like network
trouble. The board reports pack voltage in every status line (~20 Hz); the driver parsed
it and threw it away.

**The fix.**

- `BatteryMonitor` (`esp32_protocol.py`): state from the **median of the last ~3 s**, so
  the dip under motor load does not flip it. `low` below 10.5 V (3.5 V/cell), `critical`
  below 9.9 V (3.3 V/cell). First-cut thresholds for a 3-cell pack, not measured against
  this pack's curve.
- `esp32_driver`: publishes `/robot_001/battery_voltage` (~1 Hz, so it is in every bag),
  logs one line when the state changes — `esp32_driver: battery 11.82 V (ok)` — and on
  `critical` stops and turns every later command into a stop.
- `robot_boot.sh`: reads that line after the drivers come up; no mission unless it says
  `ok`. A missing line counts as bad. `ROBOT_BOOT_IGNORE_BATTERY=1` overrides.

**Verified.** Unit tests for thresholds, median, the zero-reading case; driver tests for
publish, refuse-on-critical, and "a brief sag is not critical"; boot-script tests for
ok / low / critical / no reading / override. Not yet seen on the robot, and the reading
has not been compared with a multimeter.
